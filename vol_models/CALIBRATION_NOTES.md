# Calibration notes: BS vs Heston vs SABR against the synthetic vol surface

Generated by `vol_models/comparison.py` (`python -m vol_models.comparison`).
Grid: `spot=100`, `strikes=70..130 step 5` (13 strikes), `maturities=[1M, 3M,
6M, 1Y, 2Y, 3Y]`, `pricing.vol_surface.generate_vol_surface`'s **default**
parameters (`atm_vol_short=0.24, atm_vol_long=0.18, skew_short=-0.55,
skew_long=-0.10, smile_short=0.35, smile_long=0.08`, decays `=0.75-1.0`).
`rate=0.03`, `div_yield=0.0` assumed throughout (flagged in
`vol_models/comparison.py`'s module docstring — the target surface itself
carries no rate). Plot: `data/vol_model_comparison.png`.

Heston fits ~4.8x better than flat BS and ~2.3x better than global SABR on

## Where Black-Scholes mis-prices, and why

BS error isn't uniform — it is worst exactly where the target surface's
skew/smile is steepest, and best exactly where a flat vol happens to cross
the smile:

- **By strike** (mean abs IV error, averaged over all maturities): worst at
  the wings (K=70: 0.114, K=130: 0.074), smallest near ATM (K=100: 0.019,
  K=105: 0.026). This is mechanical: `generate_vol_surface`'s IV is
  quadratic in log-moneyness, `iv = atm + skew*m + smile*m^2`, so a flat
  line can only ever match it at (at most) the 1-2 strikes where the
  quadratic crosses the flat level — everywhere else the gap grows with
  `|m|`, worse on the put wing than the call wing because `skew < 0`
  compounds with the `smile*m^2` term on that side.
- **By maturity** (mean abs IV error): worst short-dated (1M: 0.087, 3M:
  0.072), best long-dated (2Y: 0.033, 3Y: 0.035). `generate_vol_surface`
  decays `skew` from -0.55 (short) toward -0.10 (long) and `smile` from
  0.35 toward 0.08 with e-folding time ~0.75-1.0y — so the smile genuinely
  flattens out at long maturities, and a single constant vol gets
  structurally closer to a flatter curve. It never gets *good* at long
  maturities (RMSE floor ~0.033-0.035, since the surface still has a
  non-trivial term structure in the ATM level itself, 0.24 -> 0.18), but it
  gets much worse at 1M where both the skew and smile coefficients are at
  their steepest.
- **Single worst point**: K=70, T=1M, error = 0.235 (target IV ≈ 0.457 vs
  BS's flat 0.221) — the deep-OTM-put / near-term corner where negative
  skew and positive smile convexity both push IV up the most, and BS has
  no mechanism to move away from its single constant.

In plain terms: BS misprices short-dated downside protection (puts well
below spot, or equivalently calls well above spot via put-call parity)
worst — it dramatically underprices the crash-risk premium the synthetic
surface bakes into short-dated wings, and is closest to right for near-the-
-money, longer-dated options, which is exactly the region a flat-vol quoting
engine is "calibrated" (via the mean) to fit best.

## Where Heston and SABR each fall short

- **Heston** (5 params: kappa, theta, sigma, rho, v0) has genuine term
  structure (mean reversion lets ATM level and effective skew decay
  differently across maturities), so it tracks the target much better
  everywhere, including the 1M/3M short end. Its own worst residuals are at
  the most extreme wing/maturity combos (deep low-strike 1M, deep wings at
  3Y) — Heston's smile is generated by a single (rho, sigma) pair per
  fit, so it cannot independently match `smile_short` vs `smile_long`
  decaying at a *different* rate than `skew_short` vs `skew_long` the way
  the synthetic surface's construction allows (three independent
  exponential decays with independent half-lives) — see
  `pricing.vol_surface`'s ARCHITECTURE.md entry.
- **SABR** (global, single alpha/rho/nu across all maturities — see
  `calibrate_sabr`'s docstring) fits the short end well (that's close to
  where a single SABR smile is naturally shaped) but degrades badly at
  long maturities (2Y/3Y) where it has no way to flatten the skew the way
  the target does — you can see `rho` calibrate to essentially its bound
  (-0.999) in this run, straining to fit the steep short-dated skew at the
  expense of the long end. This is the direct, visible consequence of
  fitting one SABR slice across a surface with real term structure; a
  per-maturity SABR calibration (not implemented here — see
  `calibrate_sabr`'s docstring for why) would fit each maturity's smile
  much better individually, at the cost of `vol_models.fair_value`'s
  single-flat-`params`-dict contract.
